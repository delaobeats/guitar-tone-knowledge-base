# Fuzz and Boost

Fuzz and boost pedals can both make a guitar stand out, but they solve different problems. A fuzz heavily reshapes the waveform, producing a thick, sometimes ragged texture that can sound almost like a synthesizer. A clean boost is intended primarily to raise the signal level while changing its basic character as little as possible, although the amplifier or next pedal may distort more when it receives that stronger signal.

## Fuzz: Texture Before Precision

Fuzz can be great for sustained single notes, gritty riffs, and unusual sound-design layers. It is not always ideal for a tightly palm-muted part because the low frequencies and heavy saturation can blur the attack. The guitar's own volume knob can be surprisingly important: some fuzz circuits clean up noticeably when you roll the guitar volume back.

### Why Placement Matters

Certain vintage-style fuzz designs respond directly to the guitar pickups. Putting a buffered tuner or another active pedal before one of these circuits can change the sound and its volume-knob response. Modern fuzz pedals vary, so the manufacturer's guidance matters more than any universal rule. Experiment with the options described in [[signal-chain/index|signal chain and pedal order]].

## Boost: More Than “Louder”

A boost before an overdrive or distorted amplifier often increases saturation; a boost after a drive pedal may produce a clearer volume increase, depending on the rest of the rig. That difference is worth testing before a rehearsal or performance.

Try this experiment:

1. Play one riff with only your existing [[pedal-types/overdrive-and-distortion|overdrive or distortion]] sound.
2. Activate the boost before the gain stage and listen for compression and attack.
3. Move the boost after the gain stage, if your setup allows, and compare the volume change.

If the boosted sound becomes harsh or muddy, revisit [[pedal-types/compression-and-eq|compression and EQ]]. The goal is not to collect gain pedals for their own sake; it is to know what each one contributes to the arrangement.
