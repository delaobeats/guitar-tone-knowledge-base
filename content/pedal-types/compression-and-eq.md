# Compression and EQ

Some pedals change the character of a guitar sound immediately, while others help it sit better in a song. Compression and equalization (EQ) often fall into the second group. They can seem less exciting than distortion or delay, but they are useful tools for balancing note volume, cleaning up frequencies, and making different guitar parts work together.

## Compression: Controlling Dynamics

A compressor reduces the difference between louder and quieter parts of a signal according to its settings. It can make picked notes feel more even and increase the apparent sustain. Common controls include sustain or threshold, attack, and level. A fast attack can tame the initial pick transient; a slower attack may preserve more of that first hit. Not all guitar compressors offer the same controls.

### An Easy Comparison

Play an arpeggio with the compressor bypassed, making some notes intentionally softer than others. Turn it on at a moderate setting and play the same part. Listen for whether the quiet notes become easier to hear, but also whether the guitar loses some expressiveness. Extreme compression is not always desirable, especially when a distorted amplifier is already compressing the sound.

## EQ: Making Room for Each Part

EQ pedals adjust selected frequency ranges. Too much low end can make heavy chords feel muddy, while excessive upper mids or highs can make a part tiring to hear. Rather than boosting every band, begin with small changes and listen at the volume where you actually play or rehearse.

For example, a palm-muted rhythm part may need a different balance from a lead melody with [[pedal-types/delay-and-reverb|long delay repeats]]. An EQ adjustment that helps one guitar by itself may not be helpful once bass and drums enter the arrangement.

## How These Tools Connect

Try comparing the same phrase with [[pedal-types/overdrive-and-distortion|overdrive and distortion]], then with compression and EQ added. Listen for clarity rather than simply loudness. A [[pedal-types/fuzz-and-boost|boost pedal]] can also change the signal entering a compressor or amp, and [[pedal-types/modulation-effects|modulation]] may change which frequencies stand out.

> Small adjustments that support the whole song often work better than a dramatic setting that only sounds good in isolation.
